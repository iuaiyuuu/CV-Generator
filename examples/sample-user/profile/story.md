# Story and personality (FICTIONAL EXAMPLE)

## 1. What pulls me

I'm the kind of person who gets curious the moment something doesn't add up, and I really like solving problems because every problem is a small mystery. You start with a mess, you poke at it, and at the end you know something you didn't know before.

At the campus cooperative the managers were guessing which products to restock. That bothered me. I wanted them to be able to check instead of guess.

## 2. How I work

### I finish what I start

Once I begin something, I want to see it through. My dashboard project was already graded in December, but the totals still didn't match the cooperative's paper records, and I couldn't leave it like that. I kept hunting for duplicates and missing prices until the numbers matched, then sent the managers the corrected figures. They ended up using the dashboard in their monthly meeting.

(Proof: `info/PROJECT/sales-dashboard.md`)

### I explain things simply

The cooperative manager wasn't technical, but needed the weekly numbers. So I wrote the summary in plain sentences with one small table. The manager kept asking for the same format every week.

(Proof: `profile/experience.md`, Operations Intern)

## 3. When something goes wrong

My first dashboard showed revenue far too high. It looked too good, and that made me suspicious. It turned out to be duplicate rows. I fixed it and told the managers that the first number I had shown them was wrong. I would rather say that than let a wrong number stand.

## 4. What I want next

I want to work as a junior data analyst. I want to learn SQL properly, and after that a BI tool. What I like most is the moment when a confusing table turns into a decision someone can act on.

## 5. What I don't want to claim

I haven't worked with a company's real data volume yet. And I'm still learning Power BI; I can't use it confidently.

## 6. Words about myself

I like being called careful and steady. I don't like "passionate" or "expert"; that isn't me.
